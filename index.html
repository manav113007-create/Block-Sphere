Blockchain in plain words

Each block contains records and a unique fingerprint called a hash. Every block also connects to the previous one. Change the data, and the chain can become invalid. Try it yourself in the interactive lab below.

Four ideas worth knowing

Where blockchain is used

Blockchain can help different parties share and verify records. Here are a few examples of where it may be useful.

Student certificates

Digital credentials can be verified online, helping employers check whether a certificate is genuine.

Food supply chains

Shared records can help businesses trace food from farms to shops.

Land records

Blockchain can help maintain shared records of property transactions, subject to local laws.

Medicine tracking

Supply-chain records can help track medicines and identify inconsistencies.

Sending money abroad

Some blockchain networks support international transfers, although fees and regulations vary.
